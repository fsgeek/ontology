## 3. Jain Threat Disposition — genuine wound, survivable only via reframe

**Genuine wound.** Thread 3 read the load-bearing claims verbatim. Jain et al. (arXiv:2503.16621) formally severs near-optimal-**model** from near-optimal-**allocation** (Defs 6–7: ε ≠ Δ) and quantifies the gap (Prop 3.1: ~10¹⁹–10²⁰⁰ equal-utility allocations; the sampled Rashomon set is a vanishing, unrepresentative fraction). Worse, the invariants that *do* hold across the **whole** set include **harmful** ones: systematic exclusion of 10–30% of qualified individuals (Fig 4b) and anti-minority bias stable across all equally-good models (Fig 5). So the strawman §6.1 mechanism — "identify inferences invariant across a Rashomon set, treat as the empirical analogue of canonical inferences" — is **backwards**: cross-set agreement is as much a signature of homogenization/structural bias as of a real constraint.

The existing positioning §2.3 instinct ("cite as a threat the design is built to expose, never as corroboration") is **right but under-specified.** It misses the asymmetry: it is not that "no invariants exist" — they do — it is that **the individual-allocation invariants are vacuous and the surviving whole-set invariants can be the bias itself.**

**Survivable, via a specific reframe (three moves):**

1. **Concede** at the individual-allocation level: a commitment of the form "property P is invariant across the Rashomon set of models" is either vacuous (vanishing sample of the equal-utility space) or perverse (the stable invariant is exclusion/bias). Drop any individual-allocation-outcome reading.
2. **Reframe the surviving claim** as a pre-registered **adequacy/scope predicate over groups/bands**, **declared in advance and tested against deployed `estar` evidence** — *not* an inference read off cross-model agreement. The band-level `mandatory_features` claim ("the band collectively uses FICO in ≥X% of depth-≤3 trees") is a `haber`/`ser` scope commitment **checked against operative behavior**, which is **outside Jain's target** — Jain attacks *inferring* invariants *from* the set, not *declaring* an obligation and *testing* it *against* behavior.
3. **Invert iteration-2 (strawman §6.1) and add the asymmetry warning.** Cross-Rashomon agreement becomes a **flag to AUDIT** ("is this a bias invariant?"), never a **license to commit.** **Delete** any framing in which hand-curated and Rashomon-derived inferences "should converge if the ontology is well-formed" — under Jain, convergence is consistent with **shared bias**, so convergence is **not validation.**

**Scope of the wound:** it lands on the **iteration-2** Rashomon extension, not iteration 1. Iteration 1's inferences are band-level and hand-curated; if they are declared-and-tested rather than inferred-from-agreement, iteration 1 may already be immune. The memory's blanket "undercuts Rashomon-invariants-as-commitments" should be narrowed to "undercuts inferring-invariants-from-cross-Rashomon-agreement (iteration 2); the declared-obligation reframe survives."

## 5a. The Follow-Up Threat Is Now PROVED, Not Merely Worried (2026-06-04, adversarial construction)

The §5 worry below ("our audit could pass a Jain-biased band") was attacked constructively the same day and **landed**. Full disposition in companion note `research-note-keystone-goodhart-collapse-2026-06-04.md`. Summary:

- **Counterexample CONSTRUCTED.** A model-band of three depth-≤3 trees, every tree splitting on `FICO` at the root (predicate "band uses FICO in ≥X% of trees" satisfied at **100%**), while every tree routes qualified minority cells (`Z=1`, a redlining proxy) to DENY via a *subtree* split. The audit reads tree **topology** (does FICO appear); the harm lives in **leaf labels conditioned on the proxy**. Orthogonal axes → predicate satisfiable AND band Jain-uniformly biased. CONFORMING returned on a band that excludes every qualified minority cell.
- **The hole is ESSENTIAL, via a diagonal/Goodhart argument.** Any *finite, fixed-in-advance* predicate P induces a finite partition Π_P of "what counts"; Jain's equal-utility freedom (Prop 3.1: 10¹⁹–10²⁰⁰ allocations) is always large enough to satisfy P's named quantities while spending slack on a sub-cell finer than Π_P. Enriching P with an outcome clause catches the *specific* band but the next band games the enriched P. **Pre-registration does not escape Goodhart; it only names the metric that will be gamed.**
- **The STRONG keystone is dead.** "Audit returns CONFORMING iff the band is adequate" is false for any finite P. CONFORMING must never be reported as a clean bill of health — only "passed the named test; un-named axes unaudited" (this is reconciliation §3's asymmetry applied to the predicate's *own output*).
- **What SURVIVES (weakest-honest keystone):** pre-registration converts adequacy from **unfalsifiable** to **falsifiable-against-a-named-predicate** — an *attributable, advance-committed, falsifiable PARTIAL test* (a commitment device, not a completeness certificate). Still strictly more than OKB (no pre-registration) or DVC (integrity-of-record, not commitment-before-observation). Residue #3 contracts: "executable adequacy" → "executable, pre-committed, attributable *partial* adequacy with a named falsification surface." Smaller than the keystone note's §5 currently scopes it; rewrite §5 accordingly.
- **The one empirical escape (decides dead-everywhere vs. inert-here):** the counterexample needs Jain's scarcity regime (k≪n). **If FNMA is threshold-eligibility, not fixed-slot, the equal-utility freedom collapses and the blind spot exists but never fires in deployment** — the strong keystone could survive in practice. This is now the single highest-value unverified fact. Two further escapes are weaker: (a) a *loss-bounded* predicate excludes the biased band only if the proxy splits cost accuracy — Jain Fig 5 says they typically don't; (b) an *adaptive* re-registering auditor escapes the diagonal but forfeits pre-registration's whole commitment value — the two defenses are mutually exclusive.
